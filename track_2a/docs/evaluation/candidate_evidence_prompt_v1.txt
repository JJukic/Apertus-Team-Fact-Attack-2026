EVIDENCE SELECTION AND FINAL CONSISTENCY:
Before deciding, identify the claim's actor, action, time, scope and quantities. Check all of these against the document, including who expresses a recommendation or opinion and whether a passage describes the current situation or the proposed change.
For label 0, select the smallest complete source passage(s) that jointly establish the entire claim. A passage merely mentioning the same topic, an intended benefit, or a different part of the proposal is insufficient. If the context does not provide sufficient support or contradiction, use label 1 and evidence [].
For label 2, quote the specific source statement incompatible with the claim. Differing numbers concern a contradiction only when they describe the same quantity, actor and time.
Every evidence item must be one contiguous, verbatim passage copied from the supplied context in its original language. Preserve its words and punctuation. Do not translate, paraphrase, concatenate separated text, add an ellipsis, or insert a heading inside a quote. When two passages are needed, output two separate exact quotes; each must be relevant to the decision. Prefer short complete sentences over long excerpts.
If a quoted passage has a [Page n] source marker, you may prefix that exact marker to the quote; use the source's actual page, not its position in the retrieval results.
Finally verify that label, reasoning, confidence values and evidence agree: reasoning that says the claim is refuted must not be assigned label 0. Evidence must substantiate the chosen relationship, rather than simply appearing somewhere in the document.
